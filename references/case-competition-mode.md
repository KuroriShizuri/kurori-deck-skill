# Case-Competition Mode

A mode for decks judged cold, without a presenter. A business case, a
case competition, or any deck handed in as a document. In this context
dense slides are not a defect; they are evidence of analysis. A clean
pitch deck LOSES here, because its detail hides in speaker notes the
judge never reads. A dense case deck is a bad live presentation. So the
mode is not "make dense slides". It is TWO LAYERS.

## When this mode applies

The user names a business case, case study, case competition, or
competition case, or says the deck will be handed in or judged without
a presentation. The router detects it from those words and from the
purpose "judged by a jury without a presenter". It is designed from
principle; no third-party deck or material is quoted here.

## Candidate order

Pyramid > SCR > Data-story > H-P-S-I-T-C. This order is a prior with
supporting evidence: the MieLestari test deck used Pyramid first and
scored 9 for win-worthiness. The methods were not ranked against each
other in that test, so the order stays a prior, not a confirmed ranking.

Reason: a case deck is judged on its argument. Pyramid (conclusion
first, then supporting logic) fits a scoring rubric; SCR fits problem
framing; Data-story fits evidence; H-P-S-I-T-C last because a pitch flow
does not fit a case.

## Two-layer structure (one file)

### Main deck: narrative, 10-12 slides, pitch density

The live-presented layer: executive summary and recommendation, problem,
analysis (brief), options, recommendation, implementation, risk, next
steps. Clean slides, one idea per slide, big numbers.

- **Slide 1 is a cover; the answer (problem plus main recommendation
  plus key numbers) sits on slide 2**, not on the cover. The worked
  example (MieLestari v10, scored 9) proves this shape: S1 cover,
  S2 answer first.
- At most 6 lines per main slide; three core insights; one chart per
  slide; no data dump.
- Ends with implementation: timeline plus an owner per step.
- Narrative follows human impact: problem, benefit of the solution,
  transformation it produces.
- The 15-slide sweet spot does NOT apply here (the norm is 10-12, like
  a pitch deck). The appendix is not counted.

Note, from the full read of the 99-item case library (25 Nov 2026):
winner main decks in 2024-2026 are often LONG (38-108 pages). The
10-12 slide main deck is a deliberate design choice, NOT a copy of
winner decks. The two layers (brief narrative plus dense appendix) are
how this mode gets pitch brevity WITHOUT losing the evidence judges
score.

### Appendix: analysis, free count, document density

After one divider slide ("Lampiran / Appendix"): the full analysis.
Market sizing with its method, option evaluation matrix, financials,
implementation detail, risk plus mitigation, sources. One analysis per
slide. Each appendix slide STANDS ALONE (a judge who reads only that
slide still understands it). Appendix slides are NOT counted toward the
slide budget.

The same analysis feeds both layers: the main deck summarizes it, the
appendix shows how it works. Never write two different analyses.

### Density

Appendix typography is smaller but still readable (body text at least
about 16 px). Tables and full sentences are allowed; sources sit ON the
slide (not only in notes). Appendix visuals are charts, tables, and
matrices, not photos (the big-space principle still applies; a photo
rarely gets space in an appendix). Recommended evidence sources for the
appendix: Statista (market size, industry data) and Google Trends
(real-time trends and consumer interest). **The density decider: ask
first whether the deck is presented live or read alone. Density follows
the answer.** In the appendix, density follows the slide type: medium
content, financial and problem analysis dense, light only on request.
**Final QA covers the writing** (avoid phrasing that reads as AI-made),
in line with the humanizer step in the workflow.

## Cold-read check

After the appendix is built, the agent reads each appendix slide as a
judge without a presenter would. Any claim that only makes sense if a
presenter explains it MUST carry its source or its explanation on that
slide. Speaker notes must NEVER be the only place evidence lives. In
this mode, notes only help the live presenter; they do not carry
evidence.

**State assumptions up front.** Judges score logic, not data
perfection. Public data plus explicit assumptions plus a way to test the
idea (pilot or research) beats a complex model with no data.
**Pick the analysis frame from the problem.** Do not force SWOT or
Porter when they do not fit; the tool must answer the problem, not the
one memorized. **State limits as "next steps" or Phase 2**, never hide
them. Judges respect an honest judgment about a solution that ignores a
barrier.

## Palette

Professional and calm (what is scored is rigor, not boldness).
Typography may stay bold, following the user's taste.

## Confirmation

The same confirmation gate as the general workflow covers the scope of
the main deck AND the appendix. The user locks both before the build.

## Evidence standards for the appendix

A strong appendix meets three tests:

1. **Every claim is QUANTIFIED** (a number plus a source, not a
   narrative).
2. **Key claims are STRESS-TESTED** (sensitivity analysis over the main
   input variables; base, bear, and bull scenarios or a two-way table
   are the winner norm; Monte Carlo was almost never used by winner
   decks (1 of 64) and reporting a probability of a positive outcome
   never appeared (0 of 64), so treat Monte Carlo as optional, not as
   the standard).
3. **Risk is QUANTIFIED as far as possible** (each risk carries a
   mitigation plan; numeric scores plus residual targets never appeared
   in winner decks (0 of 64), so a score is an aspiration, not a
   requirement).

Structure the analysis to be MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively
exhaustive) as far as it goes. No winner deck claims MECE explicitly.
A recommendation may be counterintuitive, as long as the math proves
it, not the narrative. This is general consulting practice, not a quote
from any party's material.

## Eight uniqueness principles

Paraphrased from the external review of the MieLestari deck (round 1):

1. **Read without a presenter.** Each slide is understood in 30
   seconds; its title is a full claim with a number.
2. **Main slides are claims, the appendix is Q and A ammunition.**
   Every question a judge may ask has one appendix slide, and the main
   slide points at it ("see A14").
3. **Named frameworks as a rubric.** 4P, MAUT, FMEA, Monte Carlo, and
   CLV/CAC let a judge tick "analytic rigor" without thinking.
4. **Show the work, not only the result.** Assumptions, calibration,
   sensitivity, and worst cases build credibility. A 100% probability
   with no visible input lowers it.
5. **Something the judge can touch.** A QR prototype, a model link, or
   survey results.
6. **A traceable backbone.** Gap 1 connects to root cause 1, solution
   1, KPI 1, and financial line 1. Give them one ID or one color on
   every slide.
7. **Dense but hierarchical.** Density signals rigor only with one
   title, 3-6 modules, a tight grid, and a fixed type scale.
8. **Close with a decision.** The implementation slide carries the
   decision requested, stages with stop criteria (kill criteria), and
   trigger metrics.

Compass warning from that review: the reference deck was only a
semifinalist. Copying a semifinalist produces a semifinalist. To win,
match its visual system and beat it on evidence and honest analysis.

## Winner patterns (from the 99-item case library, read in full 25 Nov 2026)

Digest: `casecompetition-library-digest-2026-11-25.md` in the research
workspace. Full-read bias check: `full-read-bias-check-2026-11-25.md`.
Verdicts across the pattern set: 4 CONFIRMED, 16 PARTIAL, 4 REFUTED, 1
UNGRADABLE. The honest summary:

- **Answer First (CONFIRMED).** About 20% of the time goes to finding
  the recommendation, about 80% to building the argument and the
  presentation. The solution is picked in the first 4-6 hours.
- **Action titles (CONFIRMED).** Each slide title reads as a standalone
  elevator pitch (Dot-Dash). The story is told three times (executive
  summary, solution, synthesis).
- **Narrow and bold recommendation (CONFIRMED).** One main idea plus
  supporting moves, tied to the case's exact target, fully quantified.
- **Structure order (PARTIAL).** The repeating order is: executive
  summary 1-2 slides, key insights 2-4, solution, impact and risk and
  implementation, summary 1 slide with a call to action under 1 minute.
  The slide COUNTS are not a winner norm: winner solutions usually run
  6-12 slides, not 2-5. What repeats is the order of the five parts,
  not their slide counts.
- **Persona open and close (REFUTED as a winner pattern).** Only 2 of
  64 decks open and close on one persona. What is common: a named
  persona used as an analysis tool mid-deck (journey, pain points).
  Open and close on the executive summary instead.
- **Numeric judge weights as a universal list (REFUTED).** Weights
  exist only in cases from 2020 and 2024-2026 (7 of 33 cases), and the
  lists differ per case. Never present one list as the general rule.
  Check the case document first.
- **Honest gap admission as a winner pattern (REFUTED).** Only 2 of 64
  winner decks admit a gap to target. What is common is the opposite:
  decks hide thin margins or below-target numbers. Admitting a gap
  stays in this skill as an ETHICS standard (limits stated as Phase 2),
  not as a winner pattern.
- **Hypothetical country (REFUTED).** 1 of 33 cases. Do not use it.
- **Rejected options shown (PARTIAL, strong in 2024-2026).** A large
  minority of winners show the options they rejected with reasons
  (scoring matrix, impact and feasibility, NPV per unit). A visible
  selection process raises credibility. Only 33 of 64 decks do it fully,
  so it is recommended, not required.
- **Financial quantification (PARTIAL).** Cases from 2020 on almost
  always ask for financial quantification, so prepare NPV and
  sensitivity. Only when a case document EXPLICITLY rules out financial
  projections (2 cases in the library) does quantification switch to
  non-money units (GW, tons of CO2, tender share plus win rate).
- **Short decks can win (PARTIAL).** 39 of 64 winner main decks are 20
  pages or fewer, but 25 of 64 win with more than 20 pages, and
  2024-2026 winners are mostly LONG. Length is not the decider; the
  chain of thought is.
- **Judge criteria (PARTIAL).** When a case document lists criteria at
  all (only cases from 2020 and 2024-2026), the most complete list is
  Matas 2022: creativity, depth of analysis, feasibility, validity of
  the business case, structure and storytelling and presentation
  including Q and A. Pandora 2022 lists only 4 items (no "depth of
  analysis or research", no "Q and A"). Never present one list as
  general.

## Verification discipline (case mode)

- **Model first, deck second.** A model or script computes every
  number; the deck only displays. No number is typed by hand without a
  model source.
- **Canonical numbers file.** One canonical JSON file per deck version
  is the single source of truth. The checker reads it.
- **One checker per version.** The checker asserts numbers, overflow,
  and consistency across slides. A new version gets its own checker;
  an old checker is never reused for a new version.
- **Read the rendered pages.** An overflow check that only flags
  elements crossing the slide edge misses content clipped by an inner
  bar. Read the rendered pages, above all the slides whose content grew
  in that round.
- **Geometry assertion.** The bottom edge of every element sits at
  least 8 px above the top of the insight bar, on every slide.
- **External review per version.** An outside reviewer re-scores each
  version from a measured text description. Note: a web reviewer never
  sees the renders (attachments do not reach the model), so its visual
  scores are inferences from the description, not pixel observation.
  Its arithmetic must be verified independently every round.
- **Corrections are recorded, not hidden.** A typo caught in visual
  check, a clipped slide found by the builder, a disputed attribution:
  all go in the changelog and the verdict file.

## Honesty rules (case mode)

- Label every assumption next to the result it feeds. A slide that
  states a price without its source is a defect.
- State limits as Phase 2, never hide them.
- Never invent a number. In drafts, write TBD or drop the claim.
- A fictional case without primary data caps at a win-worthiness score
  of 9. Never promise a 10. The ceiling is a property of the case, not
  a design defect.
- A model that is wrong in structure (for example a cost modeled as a
  percentage of price when the case needs a fixed cost) is a structural
  defect, not a tuning problem. Fix the model, rebuild the numbers,
  re-check every slide that used them.

## Worked example: MieLestari deck, v10 FINAL

The test that proved this mode. Fictional case: PT MieLestari Tbk,
instant-noodle maker number 3 (fictive), massal brand Rp 3.000 per pack.
Decision: enter the healthy premium segment ("Lestari Sehat", Rp 7.500
per pack) or defend massal. Options: A build Rp 180 billion, B defend
Rp 60 billion, C partner Rp 90 billion. Audience: case-competition
judges reading cold. Language: Indonesian.

- **Result:** 43 slides (12 main plus 1 divider plus 30 appendix).
  External review (Sonnet 5.5, 8 rounds): win-worthiness 9. Total review
  cost 9.61 credits. Finalized 25 Nov 2026 on the user's choice
  ("finalize v10 as it is").
- **Final package:** `~/workspace/your_files/mielestari-deck-final/`
  (PDF, HTML, model spreadsheet, canonical numbers, changelog,
  round-8 verdict, structure document).
- **Model repo:** github.com/KuroriShizuri/mielestari-model, tag
  `final` (v10 commit `3c8b4b0152c5`).
- **Review session:** https://app.kiro.dev/session/6cd53cd1-7737-4882-bebb-6e93ab522fd7
- **Structure document:** `mielestari-deck-FINAL-cara-susun.md` in the
  research workspace (architecture, visual system, 43-slide map,
  number backbone, verification discipline, known residuals).

**Main deck map (S1-S12):** S1 cover; S2 answer first ("Lestari Sehat
turns a flat market into Rp 641 billion of value"); S3 size of the gap
(Rp 703 billion); S4 diagnosis (3 KPI failures into 9 root causes);
S5 solution (9 roots mapped to 3 arms, MECE); S6 competition map; S7
segments; S8 customer journey (5 steps); S9 product (one pack, three
lab-provable claims); S10 financials (NPV A Rp 641 billion, 9x option
B, survives one hit not two); S11 Gate 0 first (decision requested:
approve Gate 0, a Rp 2 billion test over 8 weeks); S12 sources and what
could not be obtained.

**Appendix map (A1-A30):** market sizing, consumption trend, competitor
landscape, consumer insight, options plus MECE evaluation, financial
model, Monte Carlo, quantified risk register, quarterly build-up,
5-year cash flow, yearly roadmap, score calibration, persona detail,
unit economics, capacity and operations, two-way sensitivity, downside
scenarios, NPV contribution per year, distribution channels, gates and
KPIs, rejected alternatives, methodology, full assumption list, sources,
key numbers, quarterly timeline, glossary, price sensitivity,
competitor score basis, trade-margin sensitivity.

**Canonical numbers (fixed-cost model, HPP Rp 4.650 per pack):** NPV A
641, B 71, C 40 (Rp billion); payback A 1.33 years; year-1 volume 60
million packs (Rp 450 billion), year-3 93.8 million (Rp 703 billion);
break-even volume 18.6 million packs, break-even price Rp 5.602; Monte
Carlo 10,000 runs P10 333, P50 628, P90 966, P(NPV>0) 100%; MAUT A 3.80,
B 3.60, C 3.30. Trade margin, equal margins: NPV zero at C 9.2%, B
16.5%, A 25.3%. A is best while any option is viable (t below 25.3%);
above that NO option profits, so the decision is DELAY, not B. General
rule: B beats A when t premium exceeds 22.5% + 0.17 x t massal. Gate 0:
test Rp 2 billion, 8 weeks, 20 stores (10 at Rp 6.500, 10 at Rp
7.500); proceed only when all five conditions are green together: WTP
at least 60%, sell-through at least 70% (42 million), intent-to-buy
conversion at least 50%, trade margin below 20%, AND measured
combination NPV at least Rp 100 billion. WTP basis: Rp 7.500 is 2.5x
massal, single source (UNISMA 2025), the largest remaining evidence
risk; Gate 0 tests it.

**Known residuals, accepted at finalization (not hidden):** S10 carries
a 704x192 px empty area; the fifth Gate 0 condition can yield "Hold"
while the other four thresholds are green (NPV +9.5 at 42 million
packs and 19.9% margin); the Rp 7.500 price has one source and WTP 60%
is an assumption; the crossing rule is meaningful only for t massal
below 16.5%; the pilot extrapolation rule (20 stores over 8 weeks to
annual volume) is not stated in one sentence.
